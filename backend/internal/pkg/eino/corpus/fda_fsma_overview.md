# Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) Overview

The Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) is a landmark piece of legislation signed into law by President Barack Obama on January 4, 2011. It represents the most significant overhaul of United States food safety laws in over 70 years.

## Primary Goal
The primary goal of FSMA is to shift the focus of federal food safety regulations from responding to foodborne contamination to proactively preventing it, thereby enhancing public health protection. The Act grants the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) new authorities to regulate the way foods are grown, harvested, and processed.

## Key Aspects and FDA Authorities
*   **Preventive Controls**: FSMA mandates comprehensive, science-based preventive controls across the food supply chain. This includes requiring food facilities to have safety plans that identify and minimize hazards.
*   **Mandatory Recall Authority**: The FDA gained the power to issue mandatory recalls when companies fail to voluntarily recall unsafe food.
*   **Increased Inspections and Compliance**: The Act provides the FDA with new authority to conduct inspections and ensure compliance, establishing a risk-based mandated inspection frequency for food facilities.
*   **Enhanced Product Tracing and Record Keeping**: FSMA aims to improve the ability to trace products and requires additional record-keeping, especially for high-risk foods.
*   **Expanded Administrative Detention**: The FDA can now more flexibly detain products that are potentially in violation of the law.
*   **Suspension of Registration**: The FDA has the authority to suspend a food facility's registration.

## The Seven Fundamental Rules
To implement FSMA, the FDA has finalized several rules that clarify specific actions required at different points in the global food supply chain. These include:
1.  Preventive Controls for Human Food
2.  Preventive Controls for Animal Food
3.  Produce Safety
4.  Foreign Supplier Verification Programs (FSVP) for Importers of Food for Humans and Animals
5.  Accredited Third-Party Certification
6.  Sanitary Transportation of Human and Animal Food
7.  Mitigation Strategies to Protect Food Against Intentional Adulteration

The Food Traceability Rule is also a significant component, establishing additional record-keeping requirements for foods on the Food Traceability List.

## Impact
FSMA's impact extends to every part of the U.S. food system, affecting farmers, manufacturers, and importers by making food safety a shared responsibility across the entire supply chain.
