# Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) - Bilingual Food Labelling Requirements

In Canada, food labels for prepackaged consumer foods must display mandatory information in both English and French. This ensures consumer safety and compliance with federal regulations.

## Core Labelling Elements
The following elements must be presented bilingually:
*   Common name of the product
*   Net quantity declaration
*   Complete list of ingredients
*   Allergen statements (priority allergens, gluten sources, and added sulphites)
*   Nutrition Facts table (must be presented in a standardized bilingual format mandated by Health Canada)

## Other Bilingual Information
*   Storage and preparation instructions
*   Regulated claims (e.g., "organic", "low in sodium")

While general information on labels and in advertisements (such as recipes or games) is not federally subject to bilingual requirements, manufacturers and importers are encouraged to present such information bilingually.

## Exemptions
There are narrow exemptions for certain local foods:
*   The food must be sold only within the local government unit where it was produced, processed, or packaged, or in immediately adjacent units.
*   One of the official languages must be the mother tongue of less than 10% of the local population, and all mandatory information must be displayed in the other official language that represents at least 10% of the population. 
*   If both English and French exceed 10% of the population, or if both fall below 10%, the exemption does not apply.
*   **Note**: In Quebec, unilingual English labelling is never permitted.

## Quebec Bill 96
Effective June 1, 2025, Quebec's Bill 96 introduces additional provincial requirements:
*   All labels, signage, and advertising in Quebec must show French text with at least the same prominence as any other language (size, appearance, impact).
*   Applies to all labelling elements and affects trademarks. Generic or descriptive terms within a trademark must be translated into French.
*   Companies in the market before June 1, 2025, have until June 1, 2027, to comply; new products must comply immediately.
