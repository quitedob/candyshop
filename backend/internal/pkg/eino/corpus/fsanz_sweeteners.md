# FSANZ Food Standards Code: Candy and Sweeteners

The Food Standards Code in Australia and New Zealand, administered by Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ), regulates the use of sweeteners in food products, including confectionery and candy.

These intense sweeteners are food additives used to replace sugar and are effective in much smaller quantities due to their high sweetness intensity. FSANZ conducts safety assessments and sets maximum permitted levels for all approved sweeteners.

## Permitted Sweeteners
Sweeteners permitted under the Food Standards Code for use in Australia and New Zealand include:
*   Acesulphame potassium (950)
*   Advantame (969)
*   Alitame (956)
*   Aspartame (951)
*   Aspartame-acesulphame salt (962)
*   Cyclamate (952)
*   Monk fruit extract
*   Neotame (961)
*   Saccharin (954)
*   Steviol glycosides (960)
*   Sucralose (955)
*   Thaumatin (957)

## Use in Confectionery and Candy
Several of these sweeteners are explicitly utilized in confectionery and candy:
*   **Neotame**: Used in hard candy, soft candy, canned fruit, and drinks.
*   **Cyclamate**: Utilized in confectionery and various other food/drink products.
*   **Advantame**: Considered for use in low-calorie candy products.
*   **Saccharin, acesulphame potassium, monk fruit, and mannitol**: Extensively used in sweets and chocolate bars.

## Labelling Requirements
*   Sweeteners must generally be listed in the ingredients by their class name "sweetener" followed by their specific name or food additive number.
*   Foods containing aspartame must include a mandatory declaration to inform individuals with Phenylketonuria (PKU) about the presence of phenylalanine.
